Why learn about money at all?

Financial literacy means understanding how money works and using that knowledge to make decisions. Here is what it gives you.

Independence

You can set realistic saving goals, choose investments wisely and avoid debt without asking someone else to do it for you. Understanding the basics is the first step to financial freedom.

Security

People who understand money are less likely to make costly mistakes or fall into debt. Learning now protects everything you’ve worked hard for later.

Confidence

Being able to handle your finances, in good times and bad, lowers stress and builds self-esteem. That lets you chase your goals with more energy.

Most finance resources assume you already know finance.

This project started with watching the financial struggles of those around me. When I say “financial struggles,” I don't mean a lack of money - I mean struggles to understand finance, from its most basic principles to the more complex ones. I hear a lot of kids my age, even the smart ones, talk about the financial aspect of adulthood with dread They weren't short on effort. They were short on a starting point.

Articles, textbooks and videos all share one flaw: they assume the reader is already financially literate. The jargon and figures of speech make no sense to someone just beginning.

First Step Finance is that starting point: a nonprofit, article-based site written and coded with simplicity in mind.
